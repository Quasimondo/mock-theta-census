Mock theta functions on the unit disk, and the new ψ*

Brightness shows how large the function gets. Mock theta functions blow up at the rim, at roots of unity, the way theta functions do. But no single theta function cancels every spike. Try subtracting Ramanujan's own partner b(q). Then compare ψ₁ with ψ₁*, the sign-twisted companion found in this census: walk straight toward any root of unity and it stays bounded.

Radial test

Walk along the ray q = r·ζ toward a root of unity ζ. For f: Ramanujan claimed in his last letter that near an even-order root (order 2k), f(q) − (−1)k b(q) stays bounded. Folsom, Ono and Rhoades proved it in 2013 and found the exact limit.

What "mock" means

A theta function blows up at the rim in a pattern fixed by one symmetry law, so a single correction explains every spike. Here f needs b at some roots and −b at others. Zwegers (2002) found the missing piece: f is half of a harmonic Maass form, completed by a non-holomorphic "shadow".

The limit formula

For ζ of order 2k: lim [f(rζ) − (−1)kb(rζ)] = −4 Σn=0k−1 (1+ζ)²(1+ζ²)²⋯(1+ζn)² ζn+1. The sum is finite: those limiting values form a "quantum modular form", an object type Zagier named in 2010.

Odd orders

At roots of odd order f stays finite: try ζ = 1, where the series formally becomes Σ 1/4ⁿ = 4/3. Only the even-order roots carry the theta-like spikes.

ψ₁* and ψ₀*

Inserting (−1)ⁿ into the fifth-order ψ₁, ψ₀ removes the spikes at the roots of unity: along a ray into any root, ψ* stays bounded. Between roots it can still grow large, as the bright patches near q = 1 show. At even-order roots the series stops after m/2 terms, and the radial limit equals that finite sum, e.g. ψ₁*(i) = 2 − i. At odd orders the limits come from cancellation: ψ₁* and ψ₀* both tend to 1/3 as q → 1, which is what the formal sum Σ(−2)ⁿ gives, and ψ₁* → 1, ψ₀* → 0 at the cube roots. In general, at a root of odd order m the terms repeat with factor −2 every m steps, and the limit is one third of the first m terms (checked to 10⁻¹⁴ at all roots of odd order up to 15; not proved). Those limits come from huge terms cancelling, so the radial probe for ψ* uses exact big-number arithmetic instead of ordinary floating point.
