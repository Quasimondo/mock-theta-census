## 0. Results and map

### 0.1 The family and the notation

- **The family.** For |q| < 1 and z ∈ ℂ,

  F(z; q) = Σ_{n≥0} zⁿ·q^{n(n+1)/2}·(−q;q)ₙ.

  It contains ψ₁\* = F(−1), the sign-twisted function of `docs/psi-star-theorems-v1.md`, and Ramanujan's fifth-order mock theta function ψ₁ = F(1). The companion F̌(v) = Σ q^{n²+n}·vⁿ/(−q;q)ₙ contains f₁\* = F̌(−1) and f₁ = F̌(1). A two-variable version 𝔉(z, y; q) = Σ zⁿ q^{n(n+1)/2} (y;q)ₙ has F(z; q) = 𝔉(z, −q; q) and contains the dual series below as 𝒲(T; p) = 𝔉(−T, p; p²).
- **Variables.** z = e^{2πia}, mostly with 0 < a < 1. As in the ψ\* document: b = √x, q = e^{2πix}, t = −2πix, p = e^{−πi/x}, and the Jacobi variable transforms as z̃ = e^{2πia/x}, with T = p·z̃ = e^{πi(2a−1)/x}. The integrals I(u), J(u) and the prefactor P are those of v12 (§0.1 of the ψ\* document), at u = a/b and its shifts by multiples of 1/b.
- **Dual series.** 𝒲(T; p) = Σ_{k≥0} (−T)ᵏ·p^{k²+k}·(p;p²)ₖ. At T = 1 and T = p² it is W(p) and W₃(p) of the ψ\* document.
- **Potential, saddles, volume.** V(w) = −w²/2 − 2πi(a − 1)w + Li₂(−e^{w}) for the growing state integral I((a−1)/b). For the decaying one, I(a/b), it is the same with a − 1 replaced by a. The critical points are W = e^{w} with the shape equation

  W² + W = 1/z.

  The two roots satisfy W₁ + W₂ = −1. The **growing saddle** is the root W with Im W > 0 and w₀ = Log W. The **decaying saddle** is the other root, with w₁ = Log W₁. Vol(z) = |D(−W)|, with D the Bloch–Wigner function, is the volume of the ideal tetrahedron with shape W in the sense of v18 and v23. At every critical point on every branch, Im V = ±Vol(z). Vol(−1) = Cl₂(π/3) = 1.0149416 is the regular ideal tetrahedron of ψ₁\*. Vol(z) → 0 as z → 1, with Vol ≈ 2π·ln φ·min(a, 1 − a), φ the golden ratio.
- **The prediction.** Π = (1 + W)·W^{1/2}·(π/(t(1 + 2W)))^{1/2}·e^{(V(w₀) + π²/12)/t}, with W^{1/2} = e^{w₀/2} and principal roots. At t = −2πi/X one has |Π| = (X/2)^{1/2}·|(1 + W)/(1 + 2W)|^{1/2}·e^{κ} with κ = X·Vol(z)/2π.

### 0.2 The theorems

**Theorem Z (the exact formula; Part 1; v18; proved, from Theorems A–C of v12).** For every a ∈ ℂ and Im x > 0, in its strip form

  F(z) = i·P·e^{−πia}·[ J(a/b) − I((a−1)/b)·𝒲(T) + p·T·I(a/b)·𝒲(p²T) ].

The state integrals are bilinear in Nahm-type sums: I(a/b) = c·e^{−πia²/(2x)}·D(a/b) with D(a/b) = H_e(ζ; q)·H(−ζ̃p; p²) + H_o(ζ; q)·H(ζ̃p; p²), H(y; q) = Σ yᵐ q^{m²/4}/(q;q)ₘ. Two further forms, (Z↑) and (Z↓), contain F̌(1/z). At a = ½ the strip form is the formula of v12 for ψ₁\*. For 0 < a < 1 the formula has three sectors:
- J(a/b) is the trivial one;
- I((a−1)/b) is the growing one, with the growing saddle and volume +Vol;
- I(a/b) is the decaying one.

**The saddle structure (Part 2; v18 §2).** The critical-point equation and the identity Im V = D(−W) on every branch are proved. That the growing saddle is the one that matters was found numerically in v18 (ten values of z, to 10⁻²⁶ … 10⁻³⁶) and is now a consequence of Theorems 13 and 15.

**Theorem 7′ (the decaying integral; Part 3; v19, v26; proved).** For every 0 < a < 1, uniformly for 0 ≤ arg x ≤ 60° and |x| → 0,

  I(a/b) = (1/(2πb))·(2πt·(1 + W₁)/(1 + 2W₁))^{1/2}·e^{V(w₁)/t}·(1 + O(t)),

where W₁ is the root with Im W₁ < 0 and V is the potential with a in place of a − 1. At real x, |e^{V(w₁)/t}| = e^{−Vol/|t|}. The sector of 60° comes from Lemma V of v26.

**Theorem 13 (the growing integral; Part 4; v22; proved).** For every 0 < a < 1:
- (a) at real x, as x → 0⁺, I((a−1)/b) = (1/(2πb))·(2πt/(−V″(w₀)))^{1/2}·e^{V(w₀)/t}·(1 + O(t)), with |e^{V(w₀)/t}| = e^{Vol/|t|};
- (b) the same holds uniformly for 0 ≤ arg x ≤ ε(a), for some ε(a) > 0.

The constants are locally uniform in a and degenerate as a → 0, 1. The contour has five pieces, on each of which Im V is given by a closed formula; no computer-assisted step is needed.

**Lemma J (the kernel integral at real x; Part 5; v23, v27; proved).** For real 0 < x ≤ 1 and 0 < a < 1, |P·J(a/b)| ≤ 1/(2√2·sin πa). The reason is that for real b Faddeev's dilogarithm has modulus 1 on the real line (Lemma U), and the contour of J can be turned onto it.

**Theorem 14 (the exact formula at even roots of unity; Part 6; v22, v25; proved).** For even X, x = 1/X, q₀ = e^{2πi/X} and 0 < a < 1, the finite sum satisfies

  Σ_{n<X/2} zⁿ·q₀^{n(n+1)/2}·(−q₀;q₀)ₙ = i·P·e^{−πia}·[ J(a/b) − I((a−1)/b) + z^X·I(a/b) ],

with the integrals taken with Faddeev's Φ_b at real b. v22 proves it for X ≥ 4, and v25 (Theorem 18 (c)) extends it to X = 2.

**Theorem 15 and 15′ (the volume rule at even roots of unity; Part 6; v23, v27; proved).** For 0 < a < 1 and even X,

  F(z; e^{2πi/X}) = Π·(1 + ρ₁) + E_J + E_dec,  |ρ₁| ≤ C(a)/X,  |E_J| ≤ 1/(2√2·sin πa),  |E_dec| = (X/2)^{1/2}·(|W|/|1 + 2W|)^{1/2}·e^{−κ}·(1 + ρ₂).

Hence lim (1/X)·log|F(z; e^{2πi/X})| = Vol(z)/2π. **The growth rate of the family at the roots of unity e^{2πi/X}, X even, is the volume of the ideal tetrahedron with shape W, divided by 2π, for every z ≠ 1 on the unit circle.** The relative error is |F/Π − 1| ≤ C(a)/X + T_J + T_dec·(1 + C′(a)/X), with explicit T_J ∝ e^{−κ}/√X and T_dec = |W|·e^{−2κ}. So the natural parameter is κ = X·Vol/2π, and near z = 1 the rule only shows once κ ≳ ½·ln X + ln(1/sin πa).

**z = 1 and z = q (Part 7; v18 §3.1, v24, v27 §5).**
- **At a = 0 (proved, v18).** The dual series become Ramanujan's fifth-order functions: 𝒲(1/p) = φ₀(−p), p·𝒲(p) = −φ₁(−p), and on the other route F₀(p), F₁(p). The state integrals become Rogers–Ramanujan products.
- **Proposition M (proved, given classical identities; v24).** I(0), I(1/b), I(b), I(b + 1/b) are pure powers of q and p times the entries (2/√5)·sin(π/5), (2/√5)·sin(2π/5) of the S-matrix of the Rogers–Ramanujan functions.
- **Theorem 16 (proved, given Proposition M; v24).** At z = 1 and z = q, Theorem Z becomes four transformation laws for f₁, ψ₁, f₀, ψ₀, with kernel integrals M₁ = 2i·q^{11/60}·P·J(1/b), M₀ = 2i·q^{29/60}·P·J(b + 1/b) in the place of the non-modular term.
- **Theorem 17 (proved, given the known transformation law of χ₀, χ₁ and classical identities; v24, v27; the two series identities (R3), (R4) follow from relations of Ramanujan's last letter, v30 §1).** M₁ = (60t/π)^{1/2}·L(2/5, 10t) and M₀ = (60t/π)^{1/2}·L(1/5, 10t), with L(r, α) the Mordell integral of the fifth-order laws. v27 §5 extends it to the unit circle, 0 < x ≤ 1.

So along the family one kernel integral passes from "Borel sum of a divergent series" at z = −1 (v17) to "Mordell integral" at z = 1, and the state integrals pass from carrying the tetrahedron volume to being S-matrix entries. The four laws of Theorem 16 are the classical ones of Zwegers (thesis, 2002, Propositions 4.10, 4.13) and Gordon–McIntosh (2012), term by term.

### 0.3 The picture

For 0 < a < 1 the three terms of Theorem Z behave like the three sectors of ψ₁\*. The trivial sector P·J(a/b) is bounded, and numerically it is close to 1/(1 − 2z), the analytic continuation of Σ(2z)ⁿ. The growing sector carries e^{+Vol/|t|} and the decaying one e^{−Vol/|t|}. As z → 1 the tetrahedron flattens: the two roots of W² + W = 1/z become real (−φ and 1/φ), the volume closes linearly in |a|, and the three sectors become of the same size. At z = 1 the state integrals are modular, and the mock theta structure comes entirely from the dual series and from the kernel integral.

### 0.4 Map of the proofs

1. **Part 1 → Theorem Z.** Theorems A–C of v12 (Part 1 of the ψ\* document) at u = a/b, plus the strip rearrangement.
2. **Part 3 → Theorem 7′.** Laplace's method (Lemma L) on the horizontal line through w₁, with the bounds for 1/Φ_b of Lemma B (Part 2 of the ψ\* document). Lemma V gives the sector 60°.
3. **Part 4 → Theorem 13.** Laplace's method on the five-piece contour C (part (a)) and on its tilted version C′ (part (b)). §5 of v22 shows why a simpler contour fails.
4. **Part 5 → Lemma J.** Lemma U, the contour turn, and the integral of e^{2πas}/(1 + e^{2πs}).
5. **Part 6 → Theorems 14, 15, 15′.** The radial limit of Theorem Z at x = 1/X + iε (Lemmas R1–R3′ of the ψ\* document, with the extra variable T), then Theorems 13 (a), 7′ and Lemma J for the three terms.
6. **Part 7 → z = 1 and z = q.** Theorem Z at a = 0 (z = 1) and at a = x (z = q), the series identities of v18 §3.1, then Proposition M, Theorem 16 and the comparison with the classical laws (v24 §3), and continuity on the circle (v27 §5).

The volume rule at even X therefore rests on v12 (kernel identity), v16 (Faddeev's dilogarithm), v19 and v26 (Lemma B, Lemma L, Theorem 7′), v20 and v25 (radial limits), v22 (Theorems 13, 14) and v23 (Lemma J). It does not use the descent inequality of v15 or the uniform bound of the kernel integrand, which are needed only for Borel summability.

### 0.6 Where a referee should look first

1. **Part 4, the contour C of Theorem 13 (a).** The margins of (L1) are proportional to the volume and vanish at both ends: sup (H − H(w₀))/min(d², d) ≈ −0.315·Vol as a → 0 and −0.118·Vol as a → 1 (stress test v2, T13a.2). The continuation of Li₂(−e^{w}) on the pieces outside the strip (Im w = 2π and the imaginary axis above iπ) must be exactly as v22 §1 states. The bound for 1/Φ_b on C holds with constant 2, attained at the corner 2πi.
2. **Part 4, Theorem 13 (b).** ε(a) is not given by the proof. It is measured in v27 §2: 20° near a = 0.3, 0.08° at a = 10⁻³, about 26° per unit of volume for a ≤ 0.3 and 7° to 10° for a ≥ 0.9. A proof of ε(a) ≥ c·Vol(a) is open.
3. **Part 6, uniformity in a.** C(a), C′(a) of Theorems 13 (a) and 7′ are not controlled as a → 0, 1. Measured: the limit K(a) = lim X·(F/Π − 1) exists for every a with 1.15 ≤ |K(a)| ≤ 1.6, but it is reached only once κ ≈ 7 to 32 (stress test v2, T15.1; v27 §1).
4. **Part 7, Theorem 17.** It is proved by comparison with the known laws, not by evaluating J(1/b) directly. It also uses (R3), (R4); `docs/addendum-v30.md` §1 derives them from relations of Ramanujan's last letter (Watson 1937), which are stated there from memory and confirmed to q³⁰⁰.
5. **Part 5, Lemma J.** The contour turn uses bounds for |Φ_b| and 1/|Φ_b| in the wedges whose constants grow as x → 0 (pointwise in x, v27 §3); the bound of the lemma itself is uniform.

### 0.7 What is open

- **Odd X for z ≠ −1.** By the census (README item 10) F(z) then grows only when z^X is close to 1. The dual nome tends to −1 there, and 𝒲(T) needs the treatment of v21 with the extra variable T.
- **Other roots of unity e^{2πih/k}, h ≠ 1, and the interior of the disk at an angle for z ≠ −1.**
- **The trivial sector for a ≠ ½:** the asymptotic expansion of P·J(a/b) (expected to start with 1/(1 − 2z)) and its Borel summability, as in v13–v17 for a = ½.
- **Uniform constants** in a (§0.6, items 2 and 3).
- **A direct proof of Theorem 17,** which would be a new proof of the fifth-order transformation laws.
- **Structure:** whether 𝒲(T; p) is an Appell–Lerch sum in T; z = qᵐ for other m. The 3×3 matrix is written down in `docs/addendum-v30.md` (not part of this document): the state integrals are dual frame⁻¹ · Jacobi factor · q-frame (Theorem 20), and the q-frame transforms under Γ₀(2) with a cocycle of state integrals that is holomorphic on ℂ \ (−∞, −½] (Theorem 21, and Theorem 22 of `docs/addendum-v31.md`); open there: the full group SL₂(ℤ).

### 0.8 Relation to the literature

See the section "Related work" of the README. In short: the state integrals I(u) are bilinear in H(y; q), a rank-one Nahm sum with a Jacobi variable, of the kind treated by Wheeler (thesis, Bonn 2023) and Garoufalidis–Zagier (arXiv:2111.06645). The transformation laws at z = 1 are those of Zwegers and Gordon–McIntosh. We found no treatment of the family F(z) as a whole, of its exact formula for general z, of the tetrahedron-volume rule for it, or of the passage from z = −1 to z = 1.

### [v18 §1] The exact formula for F(z)

Notation of v12: Im x > 0, b = √x, q = e^{2πix}, p = e^{−πi/x}; I(u), J(u), 𝒲, 𝒢₀, 𝒢₁, D(u), c, P as there. Put u = a/b, so that e^{2πiub} = z = e^{2πia}, and

  ζ = e^{πia},  ζ̃ = e^{πia/x},  z̃ = ζ̃² = e^{2πia/x},  T = p·z̃.

(T is the variable called t in v12; from v13 on, t = −2πix.) Ψ of v12 is F, and Ψ̌(v) = F̌(v) := Σ q^{n²+n} vⁿ/(−q;q)ₙ.

**Theorem Z (proved: v12 Theorems A, B, C at u = a/b).** For every a ∈ ℂ:

- (Z↑) F(z) = i·P·e^{−πia}·[ J(a/b) + I(a/b)·(𝒲(T/p²) − 1) − (T/p)·I((a+1)/b)·𝒲(T) ]
- (Z↓) F̌(1/z) = −2i·P·e^{πia}·[ J(a/b) − I(a/b)·𝒢₀(T) + I((a+1)/b)·𝒢₁(T) ]
- (strip form) F(z) = i·P·e^{−πia}·[ J(a/b) − I((a−1)/b)·𝒲(T) + p·T·I(a/b)·𝒲(p²T) ]
- I(a/b) = c·e^{−πia²/(2x)}·D(a/b), with D(a/b) = H_e(ζ; q)·H(−ζ̃p; p²) + H_o(ζ; q)·H(ζ̃p; p²),

where H(y; q) = Σ yᵐ q^{m²/4}/(q;q)ₘ (v6) and H_e, H_o are its even and odd parts in y. Replacing a by a ± 1 replaces (ζ, ζ̃) by (−ζ, ζ̃·p^{∓1}).

Proof. (Z↑), (Z↓) are (A↑), (A↓) with (B2), solved for the residue term. Strip form: Σ_{m≥1} (−1)ᵐ I(u − m/b) = −I(u′) − Σ_{m≥1} (−1)ᵐ I(u′ − m/b) with u′ = u − 1/b, and (B2) at u′, whose dual variable is T·p². The formula for D is its definition, split by the parity of k. ∎

Remarks.

- **Why the strip.** |T| = |p|^{1−2·Re a} (for real a). For 0 < a < 1 all terms of 𝒲(T) and 𝒲(p²T) beyond the first are small, of relative size |p|^{3−2a} and |p|^{5−2a}; and the kernel e^{2πas}/(1 + e^{2πs}) of J(a/b) is a window, decaying on both sides. Every a gives a valid formula; a and a + 1 give two formulas for the same F(z), related by the recursion (B1).
- **a = ½** (z = −1): T = 1, and the strip form is the formula of v11/v12 for ψ₁\*.
- **The dual function is of the same family.** 𝔉(z, y; q) := Σ zⁿ q^{n(n+1)/2} (y;q)ₙ. Then F(z; q) = 𝔉(z, −q; q) and 𝒲(T; p) = 𝔉(−T, p; p²). The second argument is −q = q·e^{πi} on one side and (p²)^{1/2} on the other: the two half-periods, exchanged by x → −1/x. The Jacobi variable goes from a to a/x (z̃ = e^{2πia/x}), as for Jacobi forms. 𝒢₀, 𝒢₁ are the corresponding series with the Pochhammer symbol in the denominator.
- **Bilateral identity.** Subtracting (Z↓) from (Z↑): F(z) + F̌(1/z)/(2z) = i·P·e^{−πia}·Σ_{m∈ℤ} (−1)ᵐ I((a+m)/b). At z = −1 this is identity (II) of v2.

## Part 2. Saddles and the volume

#### [v18 §2.1] Potential and critical points (derivation; the identity for Im V is proved)

In the variable w = 2π·b·y (v13) the integrand of I((a+k)/b) is e^{V_k(w)/t}·(1 + O(t)) where the dual factor of 1/Φ_b is negligible, with

  V_k(w) = −w²/2 − 2πi·(a + k)·w + Li₂(−e^{w}),  t = −2πix.

- **Critical points.** V_k′ = −w − 2πi(a + k) − log(1 + e^{w}) = 0 gives, for W = e^{w}: W² + W = 1/z, for every k. This is the shape equation of the brief. Conversely, for a root W and any integer k, w_k = −Log(1 + W) − 2πi(a + k) is a logarithm of W and a critical point of V_k.
- **Heights (proved, for real a).** With principal Li₂ and Log: Im V_k(w_k) = Im Li₂(−W) − Re w·(Im w + 2π(a + k)) = Im Li₂(−W) + ln|W|·arg(1 + W) = D(−W), the Bloch–Wigner function, for every k. The two roots satisfy W₁ + W₂ = −1, so −W₂ = 1 − (−W₁) and D(−W₂) = −D(−W₁). On |q| = 1 (t = −i|t|) the size of e^{V/t} is e^{−Im V/|t|}. So **every critical point on every branch has height +Vol(z) or −Vol(z)**, Vol(z) = |D(−W)|; +Vol belongs to the root in the upper half-plane.
- **The same potential from the sum.** At q = e^{2πi/X} the n-th term of F is e^{(V₀(w) + π²/12)/t}·(W(1 + W)/2)^{1/2}·(1 + O(t)) at w = −t·n ∈ [0, iπ]; Poisson summation over n produces the branches k, and these are the terms I((a − m)/b) of Theorem A (k = −m).

#### [v18 §2.2] The selected saddle (verified)

**Rule.** W = the root of W² + W = 1/z with Im W > 0; w = Log W (so 0 < Im w < π: the saddle lies in the strip over the summation segment); k is then fixed by the critical point equation. For 0 < a < 1 this gives k = −1 in all cases tried.

**Prediction** (one-loop saddle point): F(z; e^{2πi/X}) = (1 + W)·e^{w/2}·(π/(t(1 + 2W)))^{1/2}·e^{(V_k(w) + π²/12)/t}·(1 + O(1/X)), principal square root.

### [v19 §4] The decaying integrals (proof of Theorem 7 and of the first half of Theorem 6)

a′ = a ∈ (0, 1). Write η₁ = Im w₁ = arg W ∈ (−π, 0), r₁ = Re w₁.

- **Contour.** The horizontal line w = r + iη₁. It lies in the strip, at distance π − |η₁| from its edges.
- **Height at τ = 0.** d/dr Im V(r + iη₁) = Im V′ = −η₁ − 2πa − arg(1 + e^{r+iη₁}) = arg(1 + W) − arg(1 + e^{r−r₁}·W). For Im W < 0 the argument of 1 + λW decreases strictly from 0 to arg W as λ runs from 0 to ∞. So the derivative is negative for r < r₁, positive for r > r₁, has a simple zero at r₁, and tends to arg(1 + W) < 0 and to arg(1 + W) − arg W > 0 at the two ends. ### [v26 §3] Theorem 7 with an explicit sector

Notation of v19 §4: 0 < a < 1, W the root of W² + W = e^{−2πia} with Im W < 0, w₁ = r₁ + iη₁ = Log W, V(w) = −w²/2 − 2πia·w + Li₂(−e^{w}).

  R′(r) = −r − ln|1 + e^{r + iη₁}| = −½·ln g(e^{r}),  g(λ) = λ²·|1 + λe^{iη₁}|² = λ⁴ + 2cλ³ + λ²,  c = cos η₁.

- If c ≥ −2√2/3, the quadratic 2λ² + 3cλ + 1 has no sign change on λ > 0 (its discriminant is 9c² − 8 ≤ 0, or c ≥ 0). So g increases strictly and takes the value 1 once.
- If −1 < c < −2√2/3, g increases on (0, λ₋), decreases on (λ₋, λ₊) and increases on (λ₊, ∞), with λ± = (3|c| ± (9c² − 8)^{1/2})/4 and ½ < λ₋ < 2^{−1/2}. At the local maximum, g(λ₋) = λ₋²·(1 − 2|c|λ₋ + λ₋²) ≤ ½·(1 − (4√2/3)·½ + ¼) < 0.16, because 1 − (4√2/3)λ + λ² decreases on [½, 2^{−1/2}]. So g < 1 on (0, λ₊], and on [λ₊, ∞) it increases strictly to ∞: again the value 1 is taken once. ∎

### [v22 §2] The contour at real x and its heights (proof of (a))

- C₂: w = iη, η from 2π down to π;

**Heights (Im V; here τ = 0).**

- C₅: the dilogarithm is real, Im V = η\*·r = 2π(1 − a)·r.

**(L1).** By C₄, the values at both ends of C₄ exceed Im V(w₀): the corner r₀ + iπ and the point r₀ on the real axis. ### [v23 §1] The kernel integral at real x

**Lemma U (unitarity).** For real b > 0 and real y: |Φ_b(y)| = 1.

Proof. log Φ_b(y) = ∫_{ℝ+i0} f(ξ) dξ with f(ξ) = e^{−2iyξ}/(4ξ·sinh(bξ)·sinh(ξ/b)) (v16 §1). For real b, y the conjugate of f(ξ) is −f(−ξ̄). The map ξ → −ξ̄ sends the contour ℝ + i0 to itself with the orientation reversed, so the conjugate of the integral is −∫_{ℝ+i0} f(ξ′) dξ′. Hence log Φ_b(y) is purely imaginary. ∎

**Lemma J.** Let x > 0 (b = √x real), x ≤ ¼, 0 < a < 1, and J(a/b) the integral of v12 over L = e^{−iδ}·ℝ, 0 < δ < 90°, with Faddeev's Φ_b. Then

  J(a/b) = ∫_ℝ e^{−πiy² + 2πay/b}/(Φ_b(y)·(1 + e^{2πy/b})) dy,  |J(a/b)| ≤ b/(2·sin πa),  |P·J(a/b)| ≤ 1/(2√2·sin πa).

Proof.

- *Turning the contour.* Between L and ℝ lie the two wedges −δ ≤ arg y ≤ 0 and π − δ ≤ arg y ≤ π. The kernel poles y = i·b·(n + ½) are on the imaginary axis and the zeros of Φ_b on the negative imaginary axis, so the integrand is analytic in the wedges. In both wedges sin(2·arg y) ≤ 0, so |e^{−πiy²}| ≤ 1. In the left wedge 1/|Φ_b| ≤ C (Lemmas F and D of v16, as in Lemma R3 of v20); in the right wedge 1/Φ_b(y) = e^{−πiy²}·Φ_b(−y)/Φ_b(0)² with −y in the left wedge, where |Φ_b| ≤ C as well, so 1/|Φ_b(y)| ≤ C·|e^{−πiy²}| ≤ C. With s = y/b the remaining factor e^{2πas}/(1 + e^{2πs}) is at most C·e^{−2π·min(a, 1−a)·|Re s|} on the arcs |y| = R of the wedges, and |Re s| ≥ (R/b)·cos δ there. So the arcs contribute nothing as R → ∞.
- *On the real line.* |e^{−πiy²}| = 1 and |Φ_b(y)| = 1 (Lemma U), so the integrand has modulus e^{2πas}/(1 + e^{2πs}), and ∫_ℝ e^{2πas}/(1 + e^{2πs})·b ds = b/(2·sin πa).
- *Prefactor.* P = 2^{−1/2}·e^{π²/(12t) + t/6}/b with t = −2πix purely imaginary, so |P| = 2^{−1/2}/b. ∎

Remarks.

- The bound holds for every real x in the range, not only asymptotically. For a = ½ it is 0.3536, and the true value tends to 1/3.
- Off the real axis the argument does not apply as it stands (|Φ_b| ≠ 1 on the real line for complex b); there the bound for a = ½ is the one of v15/v16.

### [v23 §2] The volume rule at even roots of unity

**Theorem 15.** Let 0 < a < 1, z = e^{2πia}, W the root of W² + W = 1/z with Im W > 0, w₀ = Log W, V(w) = −w²/2 − 2πi(a − 1)w + Li₂(−e^{w}), Vol(z) = −Im V(w₀) = |D(−W)| > 0. For even X and t = −2πi/X:

  F(z; e^{2πi/X}) = (1 + W)·W^{1/2}·(π/(t(1 + 2W)))^{1/2}·e^{(V(w₀) + π²/12)/t}·(1 + O(1/X)) + E,  |E| ≤ 1/(2√2·sin πa) + O(X^{1/2}·e^{−Vol(z)·X/2π}),

with principal roots and W^{1/2} = e^{w₀/2}. The first term has modulus |1 + W|·|W|^{1/2}·(X/(2|1 + 2W|))^{1/2}·e^{Vol(z)·X/2π}, so E is exponentially smaller, and

  lim_{X→∞, X even} (1/X)·log|F(z; e^{2πi/X})| = Vol(z)/2π.

Proof. Theorem 14 of v22: F(z; q₀) = i·P·e^{−πia}·[J(a/b) − I((a−1)/b) + z^X·I(a/b)] at x = 1/X.

- −i·P·e^{−πia}·I((a−1)/b) is the first term, by Theorem 13 (a) of v22.
- |i·P·e^{−πia}·J(a/b)| ≤ 1/(2√2·sin πa) by Lemma J.
- |P·I(a/b)| = O(X^{1/2}·e^{−Vol(z)·X/2π}) by Theorem 7 of v19 at real x (its saddle value has Im V = +Vol(z)), and |z^X| = 1. ∎

So for every z on the unit circle other than 1, the finite sums F(z; e^{2πi/X}) at even X grow at the rate of an ideal tetrahedron's volume, with the shape W determined by z·W·(1 + W) = 1; the constant and the phase are those verified in v18 §2.2. The constants in the O-terms depend on a and degenerate as z → 1, where Vol(z) → 0 and the bound for E blows up.

### [v27 §1] Theorem 15 with X·Vol as the parameter

Notation of v22, v23: 0 < a < 1, z = e^{2πia}, W the root of W² + W = 1/z with Im W > 0 (the other root is −1 − W), w₀ = Log W, Vol = Vol(z) = −Im V(w₀), X even, t = −2πi/X, and

**Theorem 15′.** F(z; e^{2πi/X}) = Π·(1 + ρ₁) + E_J + E_dec, where

- (i) |Π| = (X/2)^{1/2}·|(1 + W)/(1 + 2W)|^{1/2}·e^{κ} exactly;
- (ii) Π·(1 + ρ₁) = −i·P·e^{−πia}·I((a−1)/b), and |ρ₁| ≤ C(a)/X (Theorem 13 (a));
- (iii) E_J = i·P·e^{−πia}·J(a/b), and |E_J| ≤ 1/(2√2·sin πa) for every X (Lemma J);
- (iv) E_dec = i·P·e^{−πia}·z^X·I(a/b), and |E_dec| = (X/2)^{1/2}·(|W|/|1 + 2W|)^{1/2}·e^{−κ}·(1 + ρ₂), |ρ₂| ≤ C′(a)/X (Theorem 7).

Hence

  |F/Π − 1| ≤ C(a)/X + T_J + T_dec·(1 + C′(a)/X),  T_J = e^{−κ}·|(1 + 2W)/(1 + W)|^{1/2}/(2·X^{1/2}·sin πa),  T_dec = |W|·e^{−2κ}.

Proof. The decomposition is Theorem 14 of v22. (i): |W|·|1 + W| = |1/z| = 1, so |1 + W|·|W|^{1/2} = |1 + W|^{1/2}; (π/|t|)^{1/2} = (X/2)^{1/2}; 1/t = iX/2π, so |e^{V(w₀)/t}| = e^{−Im V(w₀)·X/2π} = e^{κ} and |e^{π²/(12t)}| = 1. The quotients: |E_J|/|Π| ≤ T_J, and |E_dec|/|Π| = (|W|/|1 + W|)^{1/2}·e^{−2κ}·(1 + ρ₂) = |W|·e^{−2κ}·(1 + ρ₂) because |W|/|1 + W| = |W|². ∎

**What this says about uniformity (F1).**

- T_J and T_dec are explicit and uniform: |W| lies between 0.618 and 1.618, |(1 + 2W)/(1 + W)|^{1/2} between 1.17 and 1.91. They are below 1/X when κ ≥ ½·ln X + ln(1/sin πa) + 1, say. Near z = 1, Vol ≈ 2π·ln φ·min(a, 1 − a) (v18), so this needs X of the order (1/a)·ln(1/a).
- For smaller κ the growing term has not yet separated from the others, and the relative error is T_dec (and T_J), not 1/X. This is the stress test's observation: at a = 10⁻³, X = 800 it finds X·|F/Π − 1| = 650, and X·T_dec = 599 there.

It is open; it needs Theorems 13 (a) and 7 with constants uniform in a, where the saddles approach the edge Im w = ±π of the strip.
- Theorem 15 as stated in v23 ("(1 + O(1/X)) for each a, constants depending on a") is correct; Theorem 15′ is the same statement with the error written out.

### [v24 §1] The state integrals at z = 1 and z = q

**Proposition M.** For Im x > 0, with ε = e^{−πi/4}:

  I(0) = ε·(2/√5)·s₂·q^{−1/60}·p^{1/30},  I(1/b) = ε·(2/√5)·s₁·q^{−1/60}·p^{19/30},

(The first two are in v18 §3.1.)

*Step 2: transformation formulas.* Put 𝔤(τ) = e^{−2πiτ/60}·G(e^{2πiτ}), 𝔥(τ) = e^{22πiτ/60}·H(e^{2πiτ}), v = (𝔤, 𝔥)ᵀ, ζ = e^{2πi/60}. Classical (Q1): v(τ + 1) = diag(ζ⁻¹, ζ¹¹)·v(τ) and v(−1/τ) = 𝒮·v(τ), 𝒮 = (2/√5)·[[s₂, s₁], [s₁, −s₂]]. Let σ = −1/(4x), so r = e^{2πiσ}.

- **Which integral.** Adding the laws: q^{11/60}·(f₁ + 2ψ₁) and q^{−1/60}·(f₀ + 2ψ₀) contain no integral. They are modular (the known theta-product formulas for f_j + 2ψ_j). In the formula of the family this is J(u) + J(u + 1/b) = I(u): the two choices a and a + 1 of the strip differ by a state integral, which at z = 1 is a pure power. For z ≠ 1 the corresponding difference I((a − 1)/b) is the exponentially large state integral of v22.

### [v24 §3] The kernel integral is a Mordell integral

**Theorem 17.** For Re t > 0: M₁ = (60t/π)^{1/2}·L(2/5, 10t) and M₀ = (60t/π)^{1/2}·L(1/5, 10t). Equivalently

  J(1/b) = e^{−πi/4}·(√15·t/π)·q^{−1/60}·p^{1/24}·L(2/5, 10t),  J(b + 1/b) = e^{−πi/4}·(√15·t/π)·q^{−19/60}·p^{1/24}·L(1/5, 10t).

Proof. Subtract the laws of Theorem 16 in pairs and use (R1), (R2) in the form χ₀(p) − 2 = 2(F₀(p) − 1) − φ₀(−p), p^{71/120}·χ₁(p) = 2p^{71/120}·F₁(p) + p^{−49/120}·φ₁(−p):

  q^{11/60}·(ψ₁ − f₁) = −(3/2)·M₁ − c₊·p^{−1/120}·(χ₀(p) − 2) + c₋·p^{71/120}·χ₁(p),
  q^{−1/60}·(ψ₀ − f₀) = −(3/2)·M₀ − c₋·p^{−1/120}·(χ₀(p) − 2) − c₊·p^{71/120}·χ₁(p).

By (R3), (R4) the left sides are q^{71/60}·χ₁(q²) and q^{−1/60}·(χ₀(q²) − 2). The transformation law (Q2) of χ₀, χ₁, taken at α = 2t (so its nome is q², its dual nome q₁⁴ is p, and its coefficients (π(5 ± √5)/(5α))^{1/2} are c±), says

  q^{−1/60}·(χ₀(q²) − 2) = −c₋·p^{−1/120}·(χ₀(p) − 2) − c₊·p^{71/120}·χ₁(p) − (135t/π)^{1/2}·L(1/5, 10t),
  q^{71/60}·χ₁(q²) = −c₊·p^{−1/120}·(χ₀(p) − 2) + c₋·p^{71/120}·χ₁(p) − (135t/π)^{1/2}·L(2/5, 10t).

Comparing: (3/2)·M_j = (135t/π)^{1/2}·L, and (2/3)·135^{1/2} = 60^{1/2}. The formulas for J follow with 1/(2i·P) = ½·ε·(t/π)^{1/2}·q^{1/6}·p^{1/24}. ∎

Remarks.

- **Read in the other direction:** given Theorem 16 and (R1)–(R4), Theorem 17 is equivalent to the law of χ₀, χ₁. A proof of Theorem 17 from the integral itself would prove the fifth-order laws from the formula of the family. I do not have one. In variables in which both kernels have their poles at multiples of i, the Gaussian of the Mordell integral is e^{−3πiv²/(10x)} and that of J is e^{−πixs²}; so the two integrals are related by something like a Fourier transform, in which 1/Φ_b has to supply the factor 5.
- **The trivial sector along the family.** P·J(a/b) is: at a = ½ the Borel sum of the perturbative series of ψ₁\* (v17); for 0 < a < 1 bounded on the unit circle by 1/(2√2·sin πa) (v23); at a = 1 a Mordell integral, and at a = 0 a Mordell integral plus a pure power. The kernel e^{2πas}/(1 + e^{2πs}) is the only thing that changes.
- **Another form of L.** L(r, α) = ∫₀^∞ e^{−3αx²/2}·2cosh(3(r − ½)αx)/(2cosh(αx) − 1) dx (from cosh A + cosh B and cosh 3y = cosh y·(2cosh 2y − 1)); the kernel has its poles at αx = ±πi/3 + 2πin. For M₁ this is (60t/π)^{1/2}·∫₀^∞ e^{−15tx²}·2cosh(3tx)/(2cosh(10tx) − 1) dx. I did not rewrite it in Zwegers' h(z; τ).
- **On the unit circle** both sides of Theorem 17 are defined (J over L with Faddeev's integral; L(r, 10t) on a turned ray). The identity holds there numerically (§5); its proof needs the continuity of J(1/b), J(b + 1/b) up to real x, which Lemma R3 of v20 gives for other values of u by an argument that applies here too (not written out).

#### [v18 §3.2] How the structure changes (inference, with the verified facts it rests on)

- **Continuity.** All terms of Theorem Z are entire in a. Nothing degenerates in the formula.
- **The volume closes.** As a → 0 the two roots become real, W → −φ and 1/φ (φ the golden ratio), and Vol(e^{2πia}) = 2π·ln φ·|a| + O(a²) (derivation: dVol/da = 2π·Re w; verified: Vol/a = 3.02021, 3.02317, 3.023539, 3.023543 at a = 0.03, 0.01, 0.001, 0.0001 against 2π·ln φ = 3.023543). The selected saddle w = Log W moves to the end of the summation segment, Im w → π. The growth e^{Vol/|t|} turns off linearly in |a|, with a corner at a = 0.
- **State integrals become modular.** For z ≠ 1 the coefficients I((a−1)/b), I(a/b) grow and decay like e^{±Vol/|t|}. At z = 1 they are the Rogers–Ramanujan products above: bounded up to powers, with real critical values. This is the degenerate member of the family in the sense of the brief: volume 0, and all three sectors of the same order of magnitude.
- **Where the mock theta structure comes from.** From the dual series, not from the state integrals: 𝒲(T; p) and 𝒢(T; p) at T = p^{±1} are fifth-order mock theta functions of the dual nome, as at T = 1, p² they are W, W₃, F_a, F_b. For general a they are two-variable functions of (p, z̃); I do not know whether they are Appell–Lerch sums for general z̃.
- **The window.** For 0 < a < 1 the kernel of J(a/b) decays on both sides and P·J should be the Borel sum of a perturbative series (as proved for a = ½ in v17; not done for other a). At a = 0 the kernel 1/(1 + e^{2πs}) tends to 1 on the left: J(0) = I(0) − J(1/b), and the two choices a = 0, 1 differ by the modular term I(0). So at z = 1 the trivial sector is defined only up to a modular form, as a Mordell integral is.
- **Mordell integral.** The two formulas of §3.1 have the form of Watson's transformation formulas for the fifth-order functions (mock theta function = modular coefficients × mock theta functions of the dual nome + an integral). I did not find Watson's formula for ψ₁ in a form I could quote, and I did not identify i·P·J(0) with a classical Mordell integral. Open.

### [v18 §4] Is F(z) a quantum Jacobi form? (inference)

From the definitions as I remember them, without having re-read the papers.

- **Bringmann–Folsom** (quantum Jacobi forms): a function of (a, x) on rational points whose failure to transform like a Jacobi form, under x → −1/x with a → a/x and under the elliptic shifts, is a real-analytic function. F does not fit as a scalar: under x → −1/x it is not F(z̃; ·) that appears but 𝒲, with coefficients I that are transcendental and of size e^{±Vol/|t|}; and under the elliptic shift z → q·z it satisfies a second-order q-difference equation (v4, and (B1) of v12) rather than acquiring a factor. At z = ±1 with volume 0 or after projecting out a sector the scalar notion may apply; I did not try.
- **Garoufalidis–Zagier** (matrix-valued holomorphic quantum modular forms, with descendants and a meridian variable): the formula says that F(z; q) minus [state integrals bilinear in q- and p-series] × [the dual series 𝒲] equals i·P·e^{−πia}·J(a/b), which continues analytically across the real x-axis (v16 for a = ½; expected for 0 < a < 1). That is the defining shape of a holomorphic quantum modular form, with (1, 𝒲(T), 𝒲(p²T)) as the dual vector and a/x as the transformed Jacobi variable. So F(z) should be one row of a 3×3 matrix-valued object of that kind, in the family 𝔉(z, y; q).

To make this precise one would have to write the full matrix (the rows for the two solutions of the homogeneous q-difference equation) and check the cocycle property. Not done.
