How a row is read

Along the ray q = e−tζ, a modular form must satisfy, up to exponentially small terms,

log|g| = c/t + α log t + β + γ t

with κ = c·m²/4π² a rational cusp exponent, α a half-integer (minus the weight) and γ the q-power offset. Three outcomes per root:

Exact: the law fits to ~10−50. Bounded remainder: the function stays finite but leaves a residual of 10−7–10−10 that shrinks only like a power of t. This is the quantum modular "strange" behavior, and it's what makes f need different corrections at different roots. Irrational: no rational exponent exists.

A row of only exact cells is modular. Exact cells mixed with bounded remainders is mock. All bounded remainders is false theta or quantum. Any irrational cell is neither.

What came out of the numbers

Known normalizations, recovered blind

The fitted offset γ reproduces the q-power that makes each function modular in the literature: q−1/24f, q−1/60f₀ and q−1/60G, q−1/120F₀, q−1/168F₀ of seventh order, q11/60H. Every mock theta function has weight ½.

The order shows up as a pattern

Which roots are bounded tells the families apart. Third-order f is bounded exactly at the roots of odd order. Each seventh-order function is bounded at a different subset of the order-7 and order-14 roots: F₀ at 2/7, 3/7, 3/14, 5/14; F₁ at 1/7, 2/7, 1/14, 5/14; F₂ at 1/7, 3/7, 1/14, 3/14. So the structure depends on h as well as m. These patterns aren't used by the classifier, but they could become a signature of their own when sorting new finds.

Limits found

(G − H)/q is a sum of two modular forms with different offsets, so no single law fits and it is called "neither". Mixed sums will need a two-term fit. Five sign-alternating series ran out of precision at ζ = −1, but their other 18 roots decide the class. σ (Andrews–Dyson–Hickerson) lands with the false thetas, which matches Zagier's description of it as a quantum modular form.

Round 1 notes

The test works as a filter, so the search can start: generate a few thousand short Eulerian series Σ qan²+bn·(products), probe each at the 19 roots, keep the rows that come out mock, and drop the ones that match known functions. Mixed sums need the two-term fit first. Formulas are the standard ones for orders 2–10; the third- and fifth-order formulas were cross-checked against Watson's and Ramanujan's identities to 58 digits.

Round 3: what the sign-twisted companions ψ₁*, ψ₀* are

ψ₁* = Σ (−1)ⁿ qn(n+1)/2(−q;q)n and ψ₀* = Σ (−1)ⁿ q(n+1)(n+2)/2(−q;q)n are ψ₁ and ψ₀ with a (−1)ⁿ inserted. They are bounded at all 19 roots, with power-series remainders, and their series terminate at roots of even order (ψ₁*(i) = 2 − i, ψ₁*(eπi/3) = 1 − 2√3·i). They turn out to be built from two pieces that are not modular on their own.

Two identities

2ψ₀* = f₀* − θ₄·R₀
2ψ₁* = f₁* + θ₄·R₁

f₀*, f₁* are the fifth-order f₀, f₁ with (−1)ⁿ inserted: Σ (−1)ⁿqn²/(−q;q)n and Σ (−1)ⁿqn²+n/(−q;q)n. θ₄ = Σ (−1)ⁿqn². R₀ = Σ (−1)ⁿqn²/(q;q)n and R₁ = Σ (−1)ⁿqn²+n/(q;q)n are Ramanujan's entire Rogers–Ramanujan function Aq(a) at a = 1 and a = q; at a = −1, −q it gives the Rogers–Ramanujan functions G, H. Checked exactly to q399. These are the z = −1 versions of the classical f₀ + 2ψ₀ and f₁ + 2ψ₁ identities.

What that explains

ψ* is a mixture, so no single modular law fits it. That's why the companion searches found nothing and the quantum-modular fit drifted to weight 0.508. Near q = −1, ψ* has an asymptotic series with integer coefficients that grows factorially. Its rate A = π²/60 and its Stokes constants, which lie in ℚ(√5), are the same as those of R₀, R₁. So the non-modular part of ψ* comes from the θ₄·R term.

F(z) = Σ zⁿqn(n+1)/2(−q;q)n ties them together: ψ₁ = F(1), ψ₁* = F(−1), ψ₀* = q·F(−q), and F(z) = 1 + zq·F(zq) + zq²·F(zq²).

Status: none of ψ*, F(z) or the two identities turned up in the literature we checked (Mc Laughlin; Bajpai et al.; Andrews; Garoufalidis–Zagier; Wheeler's thesis, whose chapter 7 treats the unsigned analogue). The novelty is small but appears real: a sign-twisted sibling of known fifth-order identities. All formulas, in several notations and with coefficient lists, are on the formula sheet; derivations are in the addendum.

Found blind: the A₂ Rogers–Ramanujan identity

Two of the functions that came out modular are infinite products with period 7. One is exactly the Andrews–Schilling–Warnaar identity Σ qm²−mn+n²/((q)m(q)n(q)m+n) = ∏ 1/((1−q7n−1)²(1−q7n−3)(1−q7n−4)(1−q7n−6)²)/(q;q)∞: the census rediscovered it without being told.

The candidate D, and its identity

D = Σ qm²−mn+n² / ((−q;q)m(−q;q)n)

Fingerprint QEQEQQEQQQEEEEEEEEE: exactly that of the third-order f, with κ = 1/24 at every even-order root. At q = 1 its expansion carries golden-ratio constants (π²/60, πK² = 8(5+√5)/25).

Proved: D = θ₄·f + 4R, with R = Σd>k≥0 (−1)d+k+1 qd²/(−q;q)k, a sum of theta tails. Every diagonal m − n = d of D is ±f plus a finite sum, and the signs are those of θ₄. So D is not a new mock theta function; the same argument gives an identity for the whole family Σ qm²−mn+n²/((zq)m(q/z)n). Details: d1151-identity.md.
