# d1151: a double sum with third-order growth and golden-ratio asymptotics

The rank-2 census (`census2/`) left exactly one mock theta candidate:

$$D(q)=\sum_{m,n\ge 0}\frac{q^{m^2-mn+n^2}}{(-q;q)_m\,(-q;q)_n}$$

```latex
D(q)=\sum_{m,n\ge 0}\frac{q^{m^2-mn+n^2}}{(-q;q)_m\,(-q;q)_n}
```

- Unicode: D(q) = Σ_{m,n≥0} q^{m²−mn+n²} / ((−q;q)ₘ (−q;q)ₙ)
- Plain: `D(q) = sum_{m,n>=0} q^(m^2-m*n+n^2) / ((-q;q)_m (-q;q)_n)`

The exponent is the A₂ quadratic form, as in the A₂ Rogers–Ramanujan identities. Here the denominators are (−q;q) products instead of (q;q) products.

Coefficients (q⁰ … q⁴⁰):
`1, 3, -4, 7, -7, 7, -11, 19, -22, 22, -30, 42, -45, 49, -65, 80, -87, 95, -111, 138, -157, 164, -196, 239, -257, 280, -324, 374, -416, 447, -510, 596, -649, 695, -786, 903, -979, 1049, -1187, 1335, -1454`

Status labels as elsewhere in this repo: **exact** = exact computation; **numerical** = high-precision computation, constants identified by PSLQ, not proved; **observation** = what the classifier reports; **inference** = interpretation.

## 1. Fingerprint (observation)

At the 19 roots of unity of the census (0/1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5, 2/5, 1/6, 1/7, 2/7, 3/7, 1/8, 3/8, 1/10, 3/10, 1/12, 5/12, 1/14, 3/14, 5/14):

```
QEQEQQEQQQEEEEEEEEE
```

- **E** (exact theta-type law) at all 12 roots of even order, always with κ = 1/24 and weight ½, with fit residuals around 10⁻⁴¹.
- **Q** (bounded) at all 7 roots of odd order.

This is exactly the fingerprint of Ramanujan's third-order mock theta function f(q) in the validation catalog (`fingerprints-v1.html`).

## 2. What D is not (exact, unless stated)

- **Not a product:** D is not an infinite product ∏(1−qⁿ)^{−cₙ} with periodic exponents (checked to q⁶⁰).
- **Not f(±q) plus an infinite product** with periodic exponents.
- **No linear relation with known functions:** there is no rational linear relation between D and these 39 functions, checked to q⁹⁰:
  - the seven third-order functions f, φ, ψ, χ, ω, ν, ρ;
  - the sign twists ψ₀\*, ψ₁\*, F₀\*, F₁\*, ψ\*, f₀\*, f₁\* (see `twists.md`);
  - each of the above also at −q;
  - θ₃, θ₄, (q;q)∞, (−q;q)∞ and θ₄/(−q;q)∞, each also at q²;
  - the constant 1.
- **Not f plus a modular form (numerical, rough):** at the bounded roots q → 1 and q → e^{2πi/3}, the expansion coefficients of D are not proportional to those of f. If D were f plus a modular form, they would have to be.
- **OEIS:** no match for the coefficients, neither with signs (`signed:3,-4,7,-7,7,-11,19,-22,22,-30`, `signed:1,3,-4,7,-7,7,-11,19`) nor without (`3,4,7,7,7,11,19,22,22,30`, which also covers D(−q)). Searched on 2026-11-26.
- **Literature (searched 2026-11-26, not exhaustive):**
  - **A₂ Rogers–Ramanujan identities:** Andrews–Schilling–Warnaar 1999; Warnaar, *Hall–Littlewood functions and the A₂ Rogers–Ramanujan identities* (math/0410592); Corteel–Welsh; Corteel–Dousse–Uncu (arXiv:2011.12828); *Completing the A₂ Andrews–Schilling–Warnaar identities* (arXiv:2203.05690). These use the same quadratic form, but always with (q;q) denominators, and they are modular characters. No (−q;q) variant was found.
  - **Mock theta double sums:** Lovejoy–Osburn, *Mock theta double sums* (arXiv:1502.00387), and Andrews, *q-hypergeometric double sums as mock theta functions* (arXiv:1204.2431). Their double sums are built on (q)_{n−j}-type structures and do not include D.
  - **Hecke-type double sums:** Hickerson–Mortenson (arXiv:1208.1421), Mortenson (arXiv:1208.1330), Garvan (arXiv:1402.1884). These are indefinite theta series for the classical functions, a different kind of representation from D's.
  - A keyword search cannot rule out that D appears under another form. Experts closest to the question: S. O. Warnaar (A₂ identities) and E. Mortenson (double sums and mock theta functions).

## 3. Exact expansion at q = 1

At q = 1 each factor 1/(−q;q)ₘ becomes 2^{−m}, so the expansion of D(e^{−t}) can be computed exactly. The method: Faulhaber polynomials for Σₖ (kt)^j, then the sums Σ m^a 2^{−m} = 2·Fubini(a).

  D(e^{−t}) = Σₖ aₖ tᵏ,  cₖ = k!·aₖ:

`4, -12, 212, -9588, 813380, -111062292, 22256357252, -6151601399508, 2242594480877060, -1042510608819235092, 601881816530615817092, -422501254237874316428628, …`

All 41 computed cₖ (k ≤ 40) are integers. The full list is in `census2/d1151/d1151_data.json`, produced by `census2/d1151/expand_q1.py`.

## 5. Two related observations (exact)

- **Without the cross term, D becomes f₀(q)².** Dropping the cross term gives Σ q^{m²+n²}/((−q)ₘ(−q)ₙ) = f₀(q)², the square of the fifth-order mock theta function f₀(q) = Σ q^{n²}/(−q;q)ₙ. So D is the A₂ deformation of f₀², the same deformation that leads from products of Rogers–Ramanujan sums to the A₂ identities.
- **The census rediscovered the A₂ identity.** Its candidate d1149 = Σ q^{m²−mn+n²}/((q)ₘ(q)ₙ(q)ₘ₊ₙ) came out as an infinite product with exponents (3, 1, 2, 2, 1, 3, 1) mod 7. That is exactly 1/(q;q)∞ times the product side of the Andrews–Schilling–Warnaar A₂ Rogers–Ramanujan identity, ∏ 1/((1−q^{7n−1})²(1−q^{7n−3})(1−q^{7n−4})(1−q^{7n−6})²) (eq. (1.2) in Warnaar, math/0410592). The census found it blind, which is independent evidence that the rank-2 pipeline works.

## 6. A decomposition: D = θ₄·f + 4R

The search for an identity (2026-11-26) found a structural decomposition. It has not found a closed form for the second piece.

- **Asymptotics (numerical).** Along rays toward every even-order root tested (1/2, 1/4, 1/6, 1/8, 3/8, 1/10, 1/12, 5/12),

  D(q) / (θ₄(q)·f(q)) → 1,

  to 22 digits, and the values at two different t differ by only 10⁻¹⁰⁹. Here θ₄(q) = Σ (−1)ⁿ q^{n²} and f is Ramanujan's third-order f(q) = Σ q^{n²}/(−q;q)ₙ². So near every even-order root, D is θ₄·f up to exponentially smaller terms. This accounts for the third-order fingerprint: θ₄ has weight ½ and cusp exponent 0 at those roots, and it decays at the odd-order ones.
- **Divisibility (verified).** D − θ₄·f has every coefficient divisible by 4, checked to q²⁵⁹. With

  R(q) := (D(q) − θ₄(q)·f(q)) / 4 = q − q⁵ + q⁶ − q⁷ + q⁸ + q¹⁰ − 2q¹¹ + 2q¹² − q¹³ + q¹⁴ − 2q¹⁵ + …

  the decomposition is D = θ₄·f + 4R.
- **Fingerprint of R (observation):** `QRQRQQRQQQRRRRRRRRR`.
  - At all 7 odd-order roots R is bounded (Q).
  - At all 12 even-order roots R has a rational exponent κ = 1/60 and weight ½, but no exact law: the census class R. That is how θ₄·R₀ and θ₄·R₁ behave in the ψ\* identities, where R₀, R₁ are Ramanujan's entire Rogers–Ramanujan function at a = 1, q.
  - At q = 1, θ₄·f is exponentially small, so R carries the golden-ratio expansion of section 4.
- **The golden-ratio constant comes from the fifth-order functions (exact expansion, numerical constant).** The expansions of f₀ and f₁ themselves at q = 1 diverge with the same Borel singularity π²/60 (12 digits). So the constant in D at q = 1 is the fifth-order one. This is probably known from the work on strange identities and quantum modularity of fifth-order functions, but we have not checked.
- **R is not identified (exact searches, negative).**
  - It is not a single Eulerian sum Σ (±1)ⁿ q^{(An²+Bn)/2}/(factor)ₙ times an eta quotient from a small set.
  - It is not θ₄ times such a sum, and R/(qθ₄) is not an infinite product.
  - It is not a rational linear combination (to q²⁵⁰) of these 20 blocks, even with each multiplied by q^j, j = −2 … 3: θ₄R₀, θ₄R₁, f₀\*, f₁\*, f₀, f₁, ψ₀\*, ψ₁\*, ψ₀, ψ₁, f, θ₄, 1, θ₄G, θ₄H, θ₄f(−q), f₀(−q), f₁(−q), f₀\*(−q) and f₁\*(−q).
  - It is not a linear combination of their pairwise products.
  - Along the ray to −1 it is not θ₄·(αR₀ + βR₁) with constant α, β either (relative misfit about 10⁻⁵).
  - The same searches reproduce identities (I) and (II) as relations among the blocks, so the method itself works. They also find f₀(q) − 2ψ₀(q) − 2f₀(−q) + θ₄(q)G(q) = 0 (verified to q¹²⁰), which we take to be a form of the classical bilateral identity for f₀ and ψ₀.

## 7. Interpretation (inference)

D combines two worlds that usually appear separately:

- **Third-order growth.** Wherever it grows (at the roots of even order), D obeys exactly the same laws as Ramanujan's third-order f(q).
- **Fifth-order asymptotics.** Where it stays bounded (at q = 1), its asymptotic series has the golden-ratio, Rogers–Ramanujan structure found behind ψ₀\*, ψ₁\*: the Borel singularity at π²/60 and constants in ℚ(√5).

Section 5 adds a third reading: D is the A₂ deformation of f₀², the square of a fifth-order function. That would account for the golden-ratio constants at q = 1; the third-order growth would then be what the A₂ coupling adds.

Section 6 makes this concrete: **D = θ₄·f + 4R**. The theta-times-mock term θ₄·f carries all the third-order growth. The remaining piece R is a Rogers–Ramanujan-type, non-modular object carrying the fifth-order golden-ratio constants. This mirrors 2ψ₁\* = f₁\* + θ₄·R₁, but with the roles distributed differently: there the theta-times-Rogers–Ramanujan term is the explicit piece, here it is the unknown one.

D is not simply f plus a ψ\*-type function with constant coefficients (section 2). The natural guess is an identity with theta-function multipliers, like 2ψ₁\* = f₁\* + θ₄·R₁, mixing a third-order mock part with a Rogers–Ramanujan-type part. That identity has not been found.

## 8. Open questions

1. Is D known, perhaps written differently, for example as a Hecke-type double sum or after a change of summation variables?
2. What is R = (D − θ₄f)/4? Is it θ₄ times a Rogers–Ramanujan-type series other than R₀, R₁, for example a two-variable deformation, or something new? Is there an identity expressing D through f (or another third-order function), theta functions and Rogers–Ramanujan-type series? Since D deforms f₀², a natural starting point is to deform the known identities for f₀ (Hecke-type and Appell–Lerch forms) in the same A₂ way.
3. What are the expansions at the other bounded roots, the cube roots and the roots of order 5, 7 and 9?
4. Is the attached series (1 + s₁t + s₂t² + …) the asymptotic series of a known function, as R₀, R₁ were for ψ\*?

## 9. How it was found

D is candidate 1151 of the `small` rank-2 census: 8,442 forms, 6,327 distinct functions. It was the only function to pass all three stages with a mix of exact laws and bounded behaviour. The run took about three days on the author's desktop; code and data are in `census2/`. The other functions that reached the end are infinite products, hence modular. 573 functions are parked with timeouts and are being rerun with the faster evaluator (`census2/sum2_fast.py`).
